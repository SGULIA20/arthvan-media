# LinkedIn brief (founder, 21 Nov 2026)

The founder's own brief for his personal LinkedIn lane, with the two changes he made the same day:
posts go out WITHOUT his approval, and the length stays as it has been (body 100 to 150 words).
He also settled: three posts a week (Mon, Wed, Fri), he makes the picture each evening for the next morning,
and the two live categories (markets, news) publish only behind a fact gate.

## Who is speaking
An experienced business leader: strategy and corporate management, manufacturing and operational excellence, finance, profitability
and cost optimisation, leadership and turnarounds, governance and internal controls, capital allocation, emerging markets and the
African business environment, technology and business innovation. A senior executive's thinking, never an influencer or a
motivational speaker.

## Categories (one per post, rotating)
- case: real corporate case studies. Why a named company succeeded, failed or transformed: the actual decisions, the money, the lesson.
- markets: financial markets and economics (gold, crypto, stock markets, rates and inflation, oil and commodities, currencies, central banks, emerging markets). Never assume a price is rising or falling: check the latest move, its period and its date first.
- tools: management tools and frameworks with a real example. Not a definition: when and how management should use it.
- news: a significant current development at a major company or group, and what it means for business. Significant, not merely trending.
- failure: business failures and management mistakes, and what management could have done differently.
- africa: Africa and emerging markets (Nigeria, manufacturing, AfCFTA, ports, currency, energy, import dependency, consumers).
- leadership: executive insights (cash against profit, working capital, EBITDA, utilisation, ERP, accountability).

## Research
Real companies, real events, real figures. Prefer annual reports, investor presentations, filings, central banks, World Bank, IMF,
Reuters, Bloomberg, Financial Times, HBR, McKinsey, BCG. Cross-check every important claim in two credible sources.
Never invent a figure, a statement, a quote, a decision, a market move, a date or an outcome. Keep fact, analysis and opinion apart.

## Writing
Natural, intelligent business English: insightful, analytical, a little provocative where the facts justify it, easy to read, human.
No motivational filler, no stock AI phrases, no emojis, no clickbait, no dramatic endings, no em-dashes.
Shape, compressed to the length: hook (a business question, a surprising fact or a strong observation, at most 140 characters),
the real story with its figures, why it happened and what it cost, the management lesson, one line of the founder's own view,
and one intelligent closing question. Three to five hashtags.

## Picture
The founder makes it. He is sent a prompt: photorealistic, professional, 4:5 portrait (1200 x 1500), one strong focal point,
almost no text, no company logos, no invented photograph passed off as history, no recognisable real person.

## Every post connects three things (founder, 21 Nov 2026, after the first sample)
1. What happened in the real world, with its figures.
2. Why it happened, from a business, financial or management view.
3. What a CEO, CFO, COO or business owner should learn or do differently.

## Three rules of accuracy (same day)
- Every market figure comes from a primary or highly credible source.
- What markets expect (futures pricing, forecasts) is kept apart from what has been decided, and worded as an expectation.
- A move that happened alongside something is never presented as caused by it without a source that says so.

## The visual is chosen, not defaulted (same day: his data-visualisation rules)
Each post gets the format that tells its story best: an editorial photograph, a data infographic, a financial chart (bar, line, donut,
waterfall), a comparison graphic, a timeline, a process diagram, a map or a KPI view. A data visual uses only actual, verified figures
of one period and one definition, names the period, the units and the original source, marks an estimate as an estimate, and is not
made at all when reliable data is too thin; a donut's parts total 100 percent. Premium consulting look: white or charcoal background,
clear title, labels and values, a restrained palette, the one insight highlighted, nothing decorative. 4:5 portrait, 1200 x 1500.
The picture must carry a business insight, not decorate the post.


## From the topic to the post-mortem (founder, 25 Nov 2026: "additional steps for LinkedIn as well like we did for YouTube channels ... You are free to choose any topic worldwide")
- The topic comes first. Each posting morning, before 08:00 Lagos (founder, 25 Nov 2026), the writer looks for the one business, money, technology or work story professionals
  around the world are talking about most in the last 24 hours (any country, any industry), preferring one with a real argument in
  it. It passes the same fact gate as markets and news. If it fails, the day's category goes out as before.
- Six openings are written and a panel of three readers picks the one that goes out. The closing question has two defensible answers.
- The numbers come from him: a screenshot of a post's analytics sent to the bot as a photo captioned `stats`. Once three posts have
  numbers, the lessons are written into the next draft's instructions (logs/linkedin-lessons.json).
- LinkedIn's own feed is not read or scraped; "talked about" is judged from news coverage the search returns.
